Regulatory intelligence / general guide

Know the rules
behind the rails.

Typical authorisation routes for payments, banking, FX, payouts, digital assets, cards and intelligence. The exact licence depends on what an entity does, where it operates, who controls the funds and which partner performs the regulated activity.

UK / EU / US examplesFor planning, not legal advice
Important distinction

SMUVPAY describes a technology, commercial, advisory and/or referral model. This guide does not mean SMUVPAY holds any licence listed below, or that every service requires SMUVPAY to obtain one. Regulated services must be provided by appropriately authorised entities. Confirm permissions and any referral, agency or outsourcing obligations with counsel and the relevant authority before launch.

01 / Routes by service

A product name is not a licence category. Trace the actual activity and funds flow first.

Which permission
fits the activity?

01 / PAYMENTS

Acceptance & acquiring

Typical route: A firm executing payment transactions may need payment institution (PI) authorisation, or operate through an authorised bank/PI. Card acquiring also requires scheme membership or sponsorship and contractual/technical compliance; there is no universal standalone “acquiring licence.”

How to obtain: Map merchant funds and settlement responsibilities; select the regulated entity and permissions; establish capital, safeguarding, governance, AML and operational controls; apply to the home regulator; then complete scheme/processor onboarding and testing.

UK FCA: payments & e-money ↗EU PSD2 ↗

02 / BANKING

Accounts & safeguarding

Typical route: Deposit-taking requires a bank authorisation. E-money accounts or payment accounts may be provided by an authorised EMI/PI within its permissions; safeguarding is not the same as deposit protection. A platform can instead contract with an authorised bank or EMI.

How to obtain: Decide whether the product is a deposit, e-money or payment account; assemble ownership, management, capital, recovery and control plans; seek bank authorisation from the relevant prudential/conduct authorities or EMI/PI authorisation from the conduct regulator; establish safeguarding and account arrangements before launch.

UK PRA: bank authorisation ↗UK FCA: EMI/PI ↗EU e-money directive ↗

03 / PAYOUTS

Transfers & disbursements

Typical route: Money remittance or execution of payment transactions generally points to PI/EMI or bank permissions. In the US, federal MSB obligations and state money-transmitter licences may apply; a bank or licensed provider can supply the rail.

How to obtain: Document sender, beneficiary, custody and cross-border corridors; determine whether an exemption or licensed partner model is valid; apply for payment/money transmission permissions as needed; build AML, sanctions, safeguarding, complaints and reporting processes; secure local payout partners.

UK FCA ↗US FinCEN ↗US state licensing / NMLS ↗

04 / TREASURY

FX & treasury

Typical route: Spot FX linked to payments may sit within payment permissions; a standalone FX or money-transfer model must be assessed on its facts. Derivatives, forwards or investment advice can trigger separate investment-services permissions. A partner FX desk may perform the regulated activity.

How to obtain: Classify spot versus derivative products and who is counterparty to the client; identify payment and investment permissions separately; document treasury risk, pricing and client-money treatment; apply for relevant permissions or contract with an authorised counterparty.

UK FCA: authorisation ↗EU MiFID II ↗

05 / DIGITAL ASSETS

Stablecoins & cryptoassets

Typical route: EU crypto-asset services may require CASP authorisation under MiCA; issuing an e-money token has a distinct bank/EMI route. UK cryptoasset activity may require FCA AML registration and any applicable financial-services permission; US obligations depend on the activity and state. Partner custody or conversion does not remove the need to assess your own role.

How to obtain: Classify the token and activities (exchange, transfer, custody, issuance); map jurisdiction and client assets; prepare governance, AML, security, custody and prudential arrangements; apply to the competent authority before offering regulated services.

EU MiCA ↗UK FCA: cryptoassets ↗US FinCEN ↗

06 / EMBEDDED FINANCE

Cards, wallets & accounts

Typical route: Stored-value wallets often point to EMI permissions; payment execution to PI/EMI or bank permissions. Cards normally require an authorised issuer and a card-scheme/BIN sponsorship arrangement. A programme manager may operate under a sponsor but must define its own regulatory role.

How to obtain: Determine who issues value, holds funds and contracts with cardholders; choose direct authorisation or a sponsor model; design safeguarding, KYC, programme controls and disclosures; complete scheme, issuer and processor approvals before launch.

UK FCA: EMI/PI ↗EU e-money directive ↗US Regulation E ↗

07 / INTELLIGENCE

Risk, KYB & advisory

Typical route: A pure software/KYB vendor does not automatically need a PI or bank licence, but privacy, outsourcing and AML obligations apply to the regulated client. Arranging, investment advice, private banking or wealth services can trigger distinct permissions. Legal and fiduciary work belongs with qualified professionals.

How to obtain: Define whether the firm only supplies technology/introduction or actually advises, arranges or controls regulated activity; assess each permission and exemption; put data, outsourcing and AML controls in place; apply where regulated activity is performed, or use authorised professional partners.

UK FCA: authorisation ↗UK ICO: data protection ↗EU GDPR ↗

02 / Application sequence

The same core evidence appears across many licensing regimes.

From concept
to authorisation.

  1. 01

    Classify the activity

    Draw the contractual, data and funds flows. Separate technology, agency, custody, issuing, execution and advice.

  2. 02

    Choose the jurisdiction and route

    Confirm home regulator, local entity, direct licence versus authorised partner, and any cross-border restrictions.

  3. 03

    Build the regulated business

    Evidence ownership, fit-and-proper management, governance, capital, safeguarding, AML/sanctions, security, outsourcing and wind-down plans.

  4. 04

    Apply and respond

    Submit the regulator's current forms and fee, respond to questions, and complete partner, bank, scheme and technical diligence.

  5. 05

    Operate within permissions

    Launch only after approval, meet ongoing capital/reporting/audit obligations and reassess permissions when products or markets change.

Start with the FCA authorisation hub ↗, the EBA payments register ↗ or the relevant US federal and state authority. A register verifies an entity's status; it does not itself grant permission.
