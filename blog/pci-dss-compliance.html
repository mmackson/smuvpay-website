What is PCI DSS compliance
(and how to stay in scope)?

By SMUVPAY Daily Articles Review20 Nov 20264 min readCompliance

PCI DSS is the security standard every business that handles card data must follow. It's not a payment, not a licence, and not optional — but how much of it applies to you depends entirely on how you touch cardholder data.

What PCI DSS actually covers

PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) is built around six goals and twelve requirements: protecting cardholder data, building secure networks, running vulnerability management, controlling access, monitoring, and maintaining an information-security policy. The through-line is simple: if card data passes through your systems, you are responsible for protecting it.

Compliance levels — who decides yours

Merchants are grouped into levels based on annual transaction volume, and the level determines how much validation you must do — from a full on-site assessment for the largest, down to a self-assessment questionnaire (SAQ) for smaller merchants. Your acquirer tells you which level and which SAQ you fall under.

The fastest way to reduce scope

Scope is the enemy of compliance. Every system that stores, processes or transmits card data is "in scope" and must be secured. The single most effective move is to shrink scope:

  • Use a hosted checkout or iframe so card data never touches your server.
  • Tokenise — store a reference, not the card number.
  • Outsource storage to a compliant vault.

Done right, you can drop from a long, expensive assessment to the shortest SAQ (SAQ A), which is a handful of questions about policy rather than a full technical audit.

Staying in scope year-round

Compliance is not an annual checkbox. Networks need quarterly vulnerability scans, and changes to your checkout, hosting or code can pull systems back into scope without you noticing. Treat PCI as an operating practice — one that shrinks every time you push card data away from your own infrastructure.
