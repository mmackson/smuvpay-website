Chargeback ratio thresholds in 2026:
Visa VAMP and Mastercard.

21 Nov 20265 min readRisk & disputes

Your chargeback ratio is the number your acquirer watches most closely. Cross the card networks' thresholds and you face monthly fines, higher reserves, and in the worst case a terminated account. In 2026 the rules are tighter than they have ever been.

Visa VAMP: fraud and disputes in one ratio

Visa replaced its separate fraud and dispute programmes with the Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program (VAMP) in 2025. VAMP adds two counts together and divides them by your settled card-not-present sales in the same month:

  • Fraud reports (TC40): transactions the cardholder's bank reported as fraud, whether or not they became a dispute.
  • Disputes (TC15): chargebacks raised against your sales.

Since 1 April 2026, a merchant in the US, Canada, Europe or Asia Pacific is marked Excessive at a VAMP ratio of 1.5% (150 basis points) with at least 1,500 combined fraud reports and disputes in the month. The earlier line was 2.2%. Acquirers are measured on their whole portfolio at much lower levels, so most of them act on a merchant well before Visa does.

Mastercard: count and ratio together

Mastercard's Excessive Chargeback Merchant (ECM) programme flags a merchant with at least 100 chargebacks and a 1.5% chargeback ratio in a month. The High Excessive tier starts at 300 chargebacks and 3%. Mastercard divides this month's chargebacks by the previous month's transactions, so a sudden drop in sales can push the ratio up on its own.

Why the fraud count changes the strategy

Under VAMP, a fraud report counts even when you refund the customer before a dispute is filed. Refunding fast still saves the dispute fee, but it no longer keeps the transaction out of your ratio. The real lever is stopping fraudulent orders before you capture them.

How to keep your ratio down

  1. Use 3-D Secure on risky orders. Authenticated transactions shift fraud liability to the issuer.
  2. Join alert and resolution networks such as Visa Rapid Dispute Resolution and Ethoca, so you can refund before a dispute is raised.
  3. Make your billing descriptor recognisable. "Friendly fraud" often starts with a customer who does not recognise the charge.
  4. Make cancelling easy for subscriptions and trials, and send a reminder before each renewal.
  5. Spread volume across acquirers so one spike does not put your entire revenue at risk. This is where payment orchestration helps.

If you are already in a programme

Talk to your acquirer early, show a written remediation plan, and track the ratio weekly instead of monthly. Exiting usually takes several consecutive months under the threshold. Businesses in high-risk categories should plan a backup acquirer before they need one. Thresholds and dates vary by region and change often, so confirm the current rules with your acquirer.
