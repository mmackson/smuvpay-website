MSB, EMI or payment institution:
which licence do you need?

By SMUVPAY Daily Articles Review21 Nov 20266 min readCompliance & licensing

Before a payments business picks a licence, it has to describe exactly what it does with customer money: does it hold balances, move funds, convert currency or issue a stored value? The answer decides the permission. The country decides the regulator.

United States: MSB registration plus state licences

A money services business (MSB) is a federal category under FinCEN. It covers money transmitters, currency exchangers, check cashers and issuers or sellers of prepaid access. MSBs register with FinCEN within 180 days of starting, renew every two years, and run an AML programme with suspicious activity and currency transaction reporting.

Federal registration is not a licence to transmit money. Most states also require a money transmitter licence, each with its own capital, bonding and audit rules. Many fintechs cover the states through a licensed partner or bank programme while their own licences are pending.

UK and EU: payment institution or EMI

  • Payment institution (PI): may execute payments, run merchant acquiring, provide money remittance and offer payment initiation. It cannot issue e-money, so it cannot hold customer balances as stored value. In the EU, initial capital runs from €20,000 for money remittance alone to €125,000 for the full set of payment services.
  • Electronic money institution (EMI): may issue e-money, so customers can hold balances in accounts or wallets, and may also provide payment services. In the EU, initial capital is €350,000.

Both must safeguard customer funds in separate accounts or with insurance or a guarantee. Safeguarding is not deposit protection, a point customers and partners often confuse. EU licences can be passported across the European Economic Area. Since Brexit, UK and EU authorisations are separate.

Crypto and stablecoins

In the EU, crypto services such as custody, exchange and transfers need MiCA authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP). National transition periods end on 1 July 2026 at the latest. Issuing an e-money token is a separate route through an EMI or bank. In the UK, crypto businesses register with the FCA for anti-money-laundering supervision, and a fuller regime is being phased in.

Licence or partner?

A full licence typically takes 6 to 18 months, plus capital, a compliance team and audits. Many businesses start as an agent of a payment institution, a distributor of an EMI, or a technology layer on top of a licensed partner, then apply for their own licence once volumes justify the cost.

  1. Map every money flow: who holds the funds, at what moment, and in which currency.
  2. Match each flow to a permission in each target market.
  3. Decide which permissions to own and which to rent through partners.
  4. Build AML, safeguarding and PCI DSS controls before the first live transaction.
