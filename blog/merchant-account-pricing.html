Merchant pricing

How merchant account pricing really works:
interchange+, blended, flat.

By SMUVPAY Daily Articles Review20 Nov 20264 min readMerchant pricing

Every merchant pays the same underlying costs — interchange, scheme fees, and the processor's markup. What differs is how much of that cost the pricing model lets you actually see.

Interchange-plus: cost plus markup

Interchange-plus (often written "IC+") separates the card networks' cost from the processor's profit. You pay the actual interchange and scheme fee for each transaction, plus a transparent markup (for example, "interchange + 0.35% + £0.10"). It's the most transparent model and usually the cheapest at scale — because you're paying real cost, not an averaged guess.

Blended: one rate, hidden detail

Blended pricing rolls interchange, scheme fees and markup into a single rate. It's simpler to read, but the processor is averaging your card mix — and profiting when your actual costs come in under the blended number. Premium cards and cross-border transactions, which carry higher interchange, are where the averaging quietly works against you.

Flat-rate: simplicity at a price

Flat-rate pricing (the model made famous by Stripe and Square) charges one percentage regardless of card type. It's dead simple and ideal for low or unpredictable volume, because there are no minimums and no monthly fees. The trade-off is a higher effective rate — you're paying for that simplicity.

How to choose

  • Starting out or low volume — flat-rate; predictability beats optimisation.
  • Growing, mixed card base — interchange-plus; transparency starts to pay.
  • High volume, many providers — negotiate IC+ and add routing to keep interchange in check.

The right model isn't the cheapest headline rate — it's the one whose math you can actually verify against your own card mix.
