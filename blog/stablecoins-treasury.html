Stablecoins in treasury:
what to consider before you settle.

20 Nov 20264 min readTreasury & digital assets

Stablecoins promise near-instant, low-cost settlement across borders — and for many flows, they deliver. But they are a treasury rail with real trade-offs, not a magic trick. Here is what to weigh before you route meaningful money through them.

Where stablecoins genuinely help

The strongest use cases are cross-border, out-of-hours, or high-frequency: paying a supplier in a market where correspondent banking is slow, settling between entities in different regions, or moving value 24/7 without waiting for banking cut-off times. When the alternative is a multi-day correspondent chain, a stablecoin can collapse both time and cost.

The trade-offs to price in

  • On and off-ramps — the real cost lives at the edges: converting fiat into the stablecoin, and back again. Each ramp has its own fee, spread and liquidity limits.
  • Custody and keys — who holds the asset, how it is safeguarded, and what happens if a key is lost are treasury decisions, not just technical ones.
  • Issuer and reserve risk — a stablecoin is only as good as its issuer, its backing and its redemption rights. Not all stablecoins are equal.
  • Regulatory position — treatment varies by jurisdiction (MiCA in the EU, evolving rules elsewhere). What is routine today may not be next year.

A practical checklist

  1. Is the corridor actually slow or expensive on existing rails — or is a local rail already better?
  2. What is the full round-trip cost: fiat → stablecoin → fiat?
  3. Who is the issuer, how is the token backed, and what are the redemption terms?
  4. How is the asset custodied and who controls the keys?
  5. Does the flow pass compliance in both the sending and receiving jurisdictions?

Used deliberately, stablecoins are a powerful settlement tool. Used carelessly, they move risk around without removing it. The discipline is to treat them as one rail among several — chosen for the specific flow, not as a default.
